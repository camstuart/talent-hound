## How the shortlist is built

The shortlist searches two ways at once. One half matches words, using the same index your evidence search uses. The other half matches meaning, comparing your candidate's statements against each role's statements.

Both halves are combined so that a role found strongly by either can reach the list, and a role found by several of your criteria beats a role found first by one.

The list is capped at twenty roles and is identical on repeated runs with the same inputs.

## Why a role is on the list

Every entry shows what put it there: which criterion or which profile statement matched, and by which method. A role you do not recognise is explainable rather than mysterious.

## Conflicts

A role that contradicts something the candidate stated — a location they ruled out, an employment type they do not want — stays on the list with the conflict shown, rather than being silently dropped. You decide whether it matters.

## Assessments

An assessment compares one candidate and one role in both directions:

- Does the candidate meet the role's requirements?
- Does the role meet the candidate's requirements?

Each requirement gets a verdict — met, not met, or unclear — with the evidence it rests on. Unclear is a real answer: it means the sources do not say, which is different from saying no.

An assessment records a hash of everything it was computed from, so you can tell whether it still reflects the current profile, criteria, and listing.
