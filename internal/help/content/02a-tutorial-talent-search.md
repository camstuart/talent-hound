## What a Talent Search initiative is

A Talent Search initiative is the Job Search loop pointed the other way: you hold a role, and you are looking for people. In this version the initiative itself is a workspace shell — its automated pipeline is not built yet — so the work happens in the CRM, and the initiative is where you keep the role, the criteria, and the documents that describe what you are hiring for.

**You decide:** what the role needs. **The application does:** keep everything you gather in one place, shared with every other initiative.

## 1. Create the initiative and its role

Create a Talent Search initiative, then add the role in the Records area: title, employer, and the listing document if you have one. The listing is stored as evidence, exactly as you received it.

**You decide:** which role to work. **The application does:** keep the role's source listing attached so later assessments can cite it.

## 2. Build the pool in the CRM

Open the CRM tab. Every candidate you have ever added is there, across all initiatives — that is the pool. Add new candidates with the New candidate form, attach their resumes, and log what you know: a call from last year, a placement, a rejection that tells you what they turned down. Each note becomes searchable evidence.

**You decide:** who belongs in the pool and what is worth recording. **The application does:** index every attached document and note so it can be found later.

## 3. Mine the pool with Talent search

In the CRM's Candidates list, use the Talent search box. It searches the evidence — resumes, notes, anything attached to a candidate — not just the fields. Searching "kubernetes fintech" finds the person whose phone note says they want to move into fintech, even if no field does.

**You decide:** what to search for; the words come from the role's requirements. **The application does:** return people ranked by their best matching evidence, showing the passage that matched so you can judge the match yourself.

## 4. Work the matches

Open each promising candidate. Their history tells you how warm the approach is: a placement two years ago reads differently from a cold resume. Log the calls you make as you go — this search is building evidence for the next one.

**You decide:** who to approach and how. **The application does:** keep the trail, so nothing you learned is lost when the initiative ends.
