## Criteria are your intent

Criteria are what you are looking for, in your words, with a priority: must have, or nice to have. They are deliberately kept separate from the candidate's profile — the profile is evidence, criteria are intent, and mixing them makes it impossible to say why a role was shortlisted.

Reordering criteria is presentation only. The criteria version does not move, because you have not changed what you are looking for.

## Criteria that are refused

A criterion naming a protected attribute is refused outright: age, race or national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, marital or family status, pregnancy, disability, religion, political opinion, union membership, or criminal record.

The refusal names which category it matched and does not offer a way to add it anyway. There is no setting that permits one.

## The one that looks protected and is not

A work-rights requirement is lawful and keeps working: "must have existing Australian work rights" is about eligibility to work, not nationality. A criterion naming a nationality is refused; a criterion naming work rights is not.

## Proposals

The application can propose criteria from an approved profile. Proposals are suggestions: they are shown, and nothing is added until you choose it. A proposal that names a protected attribute is refused on the way in, the same as one you typed.
