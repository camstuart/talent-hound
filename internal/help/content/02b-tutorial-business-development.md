## What a Business Development initiative is

Business Development is about the demand side: companies that hire, and the people inside them who decide. In this version the initiative is a workspace shell — its automated pipeline is not built yet — so the work happens in the CRM, and the initiative marks the campaign you are running.

**You decide:** which market or accounts to work. **The application does:** keep companies and contacts as shared records that outlive any one campaign.

## 1. Record the companies

In the CRM tab, switch to Companies and add the accounts you are working. A company record is deliberately minimal: enough to hang contacts off and to name a role's employer.

**You decide:** which companies matter. **The application does:** validate what you enter and keep it shared across initiatives.

## 2. Record the people

Switch to Contacts and add the people you know at each company — hiring managers, internal recruiters, founders. A contact belongs to a company, so the warm path is visible: when a role appears at a company, you can see at once whether you know anyone there.

**You decide:** who is worth knowing. **The application does:** count and list your contacts at each company.

## 3. Log every touch

Open a contact and log interactions: the coffee, the call, the email thread that went quiet. Attach documents to companies — a org chart, a hiring plan — and they become searchable evidence like any other.

**You decide:** what happened and what it meant. **The application does:** keep the history in order, so the next person to open the record sees the relationship, not just a name.

## 4. Use the history

Before an approach, read the timeline. The record of what was said last time is the difference between a warm follow-up and an accidental cold call. Notes you logged months ago surface in searches too, because they are evidence.

**You decide:** the approach. **The application does:** never contact anyone — there is no send button anywhere in the application.
