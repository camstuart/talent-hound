## What the CRM tab is

The CRM tab is the whole pool in one place: every candidate, company, contact, and role, across all initiatives. Records are shared — an initiative points at them, and deleting the initiative never removes them. The left side is for finding a record; the right side is everything about the one you selected.

## Finding people: two searches, on purpose

The Filter box answers "who matches these facts": it searches the typed fields — name, email, location — and, for candidates, filters by work rights, employment type, and availability. The Talent search box answers a different question: "whose evidence talks about this". It searches resumes and interaction notes, and returns people ranked by their best matching passage, with that passage shown so you can judge the match. Use the filter when you know who you are looking for; use talent search when you know what you are looking for.

## Creating and editing records

The New button creates a record of the current type. Selecting a record opens its details on the right, where every field is editable and saved through the same validation as creation — a malformed email is refused, not stored.

## Attaching evidence

The Artifacts section of a selected record holds its documents: resumes on candidates, listings on roles, hiring plans on companies. A document's bytes are never modified, its type is detected from the content rather than the file name, and uploading the identical file to the same record twice is refused.

## Logging interactions

The History section records what happened: calls, meetings, emails, notes, and outcomes — a placement, an application, a rejection. An outcome names the role it is about; any interaction can also name a role and the initiative it happened under. The date is when it happened, which is not always today.

**You decide:** what is worth recording. **The application does:** turn every note into searchable evidence, so "wants to move into fintech" said on a phone call is findable by talent search next year.

## Editing and deleting notes

Your notes are your own words, so they stay editable — unlike documents, which are evidence and never change. Editing a note replaces its searchable copy, so search always reflects the current wording. Deleting an interaction removes its evidence everywhere. The note's evidence copy in the record's artifact list belongs to the interaction: it cannot be renamed or detached on its own.

## Why the history matters

Every interaction builds the record you will rely on next time: talent search reads the notes, and a candidate's timeline of placements and rejections is intelligence for the next search on their behalf. The CRM is not a side ledger — it is where the pool becomes worth more every time you use it.
